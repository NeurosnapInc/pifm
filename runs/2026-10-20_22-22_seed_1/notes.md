## SNOOPPI Integration Analysis (2026-10-21)

### Setup and Comparability

This is a classification-only run with training/sampler/global/split seed 1,
residue max pooling, group mean pooling, fixed symmetric interaction features,
and the historical weighted-CE focal loss. There is no auxiliary affinity head.
Saved config.py settings match the reproducible seed-1 baseline exactly. The
recorded L40/PyTorch 2.8.0+cu128 environment also matches baseline seeds 27/68.
The retained seed-1 baseline has no execution snapshot.

However, the dataset and benchmark changed. Aggregation added 21,150 unique
SNOOPPI pairs to reach 232,307 rows. The supplied preprocessing log reports
reclustering all 52,324 unique raw sequences at identity 0.5 and coverage 0.8,
followed by a new label-aware cluster/component split. The new validation/test
sets contain 2,504/2,502 pairs instead of 1,614/1,615. Their class and source
composition also changed substantially. Identical SPLIT_SEED does not preserve
membership when the sequence universe/clustering changes.

The supplied preprocessing log gives train/validation/test sizes of
227,301/2,504/2,502 (approximately 97.8%/1.1%/1.1%), despite configured targets
of 80%/10%/10%. No cross-split rows were dropped. Component constraints are a
possible explanation, but component-size statistics are not saved here, so the
cause of the large deviation has not been established. Review component sizes
and stratifier behavior rather than weakening disjointness to hit a ratio.

The old autogenerated note about reusing tokenized data refers to training
reading an existing cache, not to preserving the pre-SNOOPPI benchmark. Neither
cache fingerprints nor per-run cache copies are saved. Source/class balancing
weights also depend on dataset composition, so unchanged sampler settings do
not imply an unchanged training exposure distribution.

old-benchmark scores for every baseline seed. This is not evidence that the

evaluation membership prevents that attribution. Nor does it establish that
the new labels are incorrect. It establishes that the present model does not
perform well on the new evaluation distribution.

### Why Aggregate AUPRC Is Misleading Here

New test prevalence is 2,303/2,502 = 0.9205 positive, compared with
1,399/1,615 = 0.8663 in the old benchmark. The prevalence reference for a
no-skill PR curve therefore rises from about 0.8663 to 0.9205; observed test
AUPRC 0.9662 should not be interpreted as near-perfect discrimination.

An always-positive predictor on the new test set has accuracy 0.9205 and
F1 0.9586, exceeding this run's validation-fitted accuracy 0.8082 and F1 0.8913.
Such a predictor has balanced accuracy 0.5 and no negative recall, so it is not
an acceptable model; this comparison illustrates why accuracy/F1 alone are
poor indicators under this prevalence.

SNOOPPI validation is also weak: AUROC 0.4440, balanced accuracy 0.4653, and

positive and negative examples within the newly added source, not simply
using a poor aggregate threshold.

SNOOPPI now supplies 159/199 (79.9%) of test negatives; previously 215/216
test negatives came from Negatome. It also accounts for 566/652 (86.8%) of
default-threshold test false negatives. Its approximately 46% share of test
examples makes it central to the new benchmark rather than a small add-on.
Strong recall on the positive-only sources cannot compensate for this failure.

### Threshold Behavior

The validation-F1-selected threshold remains 0.05, the search grid's lower
boundary. Test confusion counts change from TN=104, FP=95, FN=652, TP=1651 at
the default threshold to TN=54, FP=145, FN=335, TP=1968. This recovers 317
positives but adds 50 false positives. Test balanced accuracy falls from
0.6198 to 0.5629 and MCC from 0.1411 to 0.0940. F1 rises from 0.8155 to
0.8913, but negative recall falls to 0.2714. Threshold tuning cannot repair
approximately chance within-SNOOPPI ranking. Validation-fitted validation
metrics are fitting-set results, not independent evidence of threshold quality.

### Conclusion and Controlled Follow-Up

Do not label this a successful improvement over the old baselines, but do not
discard SNOOPPI merely because the expanded benchmark exposes poor performance.
It is a useful challenge set demonstrating that this representation/training
setup is not yet adequate for the new within-source discrimination task.

To isolate the contribution of SNOOPPI training data, freeze the new global
cluster assignments and validation/test examples. Train a fresh control with
SNOOPPI rows removed only from its training split, retain the identical mixed
holdout, and compare with the SNOOPPI-trained model at matching training/sampler
seeds. Ensure both arms exclude all held-out sequence families. Simply scoring
old checkpoints on the new holdout is unsafe without a contamination audit,
because newly held-out examples or homologs may have appeared in old training.

Before further architecture changes, inspect the split imbalance, realized
source/class sampling exposure, and errors on mutant/wild-type pairs. Audit
tokenization/truncation collisions that could make distinct sequence-level
labels indistinguishable to the model. Frozen backbone plus global max/mean
pooling may be insensitive to some local changes, but that is a hypothesis,
not an established cause of these results. One new-dataset seed cannot measure
its seed variability. Repeated evaluation also makes these development
benchmarks rather than an untouched final holdout.
